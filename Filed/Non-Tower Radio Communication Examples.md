These are [[Radio Communication Examples|example radio calls]] for an arrival and departure at a non-tower airport. The exact calls will vary depending on the circumstances. Each call should include your location and altitude, and what you are doing next.

>[!tip]
> When things are confusing or non-standard, it’s OK to get "conversational" on the frequency to make your intentions clear, but try to keep the calls concise.

> [!warning]
> Notice these are "self-announce", where you say where you are and what you're doing. Statements like "any traffic in the area please advise" is poor form.

# Example Calls

## Weather and Traffic Listening
Roughly 30-15nm out, begin listening to [[CTAF]] and [[AWOS]]/[[ASOS]].^[You may need a second radio for this if currently on flight following. If you have a single radio, you can request a temporary frequency change to listen to weather.]

Determine wind direction and runway in use, as well as any hazards like parachuting operations.

## Awareness Call

10 miles out, inbound for landing

> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, ten miles northeast, 3000 feet, inbound for landing, Watsonville"

This is meant to inform local traffic where you are and that you're intending to land.

## Intention Call
5 miles out, closer in, when you have determined what runway other traffic is using

> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, five miles northeast, will cross midfield at two thousand two hundred and will enter on the forty-five, runway two zero, Watsonville"

This is meant to tell local traffic where you are, and how you'll enter the pattern.

## Crossing Midfield


### Maneuver to the 45
If flying above the pattern, then heading outbound before coming back on the 45:

> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, overhead midfield at two thousand two hundred, will enter on the forty-five, runway two zero, Watsonville"

Plan to cross at least 500' above pattern altitude, although 1000' above may be preferred to avoid turbine pattern altitude.

> [!warning] 
> You may hear pilots call this a "teardrop" entry. This name and the drawing in the [[AFH Ch8]] imply a tight, descending turn. But this generally unsafe and does not give time to [[see and avoid]] traffic when trying to enter into the pattern.

### Direct Entry
If using direct entry (also called the "*alternate midfield entry*") instead:

> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, crossing midfield at one thousand two hundred, will enter direct downwind, runway two zero, Watsonville"

## The pattern

### On the 45
After crossing midfield, fly outbound about 3 miles, descend to pattern altitude, then turn back onto the 45

> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, on the forty-five, runway two zero, Watsonville"

> [!note]
> If you are on the 45 or downwind, you are assumed to be at pattern altitude, unless you call out something different.

### On Downwind
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, left downwind, runway two zero, Watsonville"

>[!tip]
> There's a case to be made that 'left' is not needed here, since everyone should know the traffic pattern direction.
> 
> However, IFR practice approaches sometimes fly opposite traffic, so it's good to get in the habit of announcing.

### On Base
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, left base, runway two zero, Watsonville"

### On Final
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, final approach, two zero, full-stop landing, Watsonville"

## After Landing
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, exiting runway two zero (at Delta), taxiing transient parking, Watsonville"

The taxiway callout is optional.

## Departing
### Taxiing for Departure
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, transient parking, taxiing runway two zero via Alpha for departure, Watsonville"

It’s nice to include the taxiway if there are multiple ways to get there.

### If You Need to Cross an Intersecting Runway
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, taxiing across runway two seven at Alpha, Watsonville"

### Before Taking the Runway for Departure
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, taking runway two zero, then right crosswind departure, Watsonville"

> [!warning]
> You'll sometimes hear "taking the active", instead of the actual runway number being taken. "The active" is unclear and a lost chance to convey useful information.

### During the Upwind Climb
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, departure leg runway two zero, then right crosswind departure, Watsonville"

### On the Crosswind
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, departing right crosswind from two zero, one thousand three hundred feet, climbing, Watsonville"

### Last Call During Departure
> [!quote] "Watsonville traffic, Cessna Tango India Sierra, three miles west, two thousand seven hundred feet, climbing, departing to the west, Watsonville"

> [!warning]
> You may hear "Last Call" in this sort of communication. The general consensus is this is bad practice, since saying "departing to the west" also conveys you're leaving the CTAF frequency.
